WHO I AM

Practical experience first. Networking knowledge growing around it.

I am a UK-based broadband engineer with several years of frontline experience diagnosing connectivity, Wi-Fi and customer network problems in real homes. That work is what pushed me to understand more deeply what is happening beyond the router, the access point and the immediate fault in front of me.

Network engineering has become the natural next step. I enjoy the combination of structured logic and practical problem-solving: forming a theory, checking the evidence, finding where expected behaviour diverges from reality and then proving the fix.

HOW I WORK

The part I care about most is proving what happened.

01 Build deliberately. Start from the intended topology, addressing and behaviour rather than entering commands without a clear design.
02 Verify each layer. Interface state, VLAN membership, trunks, addressing, routes, neighbour relationships, services and policy all need evidence.
03 Troubleshoot methodically. I try to identify where observed behaviour stops matching the design rather than making several configuration changes at once.
04 Document the mistake as well as the fix. Failed attempts are often the most useful part of a lab because they show how the fault was isolated and what I learned from it.
